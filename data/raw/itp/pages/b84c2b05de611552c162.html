Welcome to the Institute for Theoretical Physics (ITP).
Some news about our recent activities can be found below.

Seminars

All seminars taking place throughout the semester can be found here.

 

The Institute for Theoretical Physics has about 60 members which are active in various fields. The ITP is part of the Albert Einstein Center for Fundamental Physics (AEC), a collaboration mainly focused on experimental and theoretical particle physics and its applications (e.g. medical physics), as well as on the related spinoff and outreach activities. The AEC also maintains an active visitor program.

Our educational activities are organized together with Physics and Astronomy.

Research

Gasser/Leutwyler paper reaches 5000 citations

The 1984 paper by Jürg Gasser and Heiri Leutwyler establishing chiral perturbation theory as standard tool for low-energy QCD has reached 5000 citations in the inspirehep database. In the paper, the low-energy phenomenology of pions as Nambu-Goldstone bosons is systematically developed as an effective field theory at one-loop order, which, together with the companion paper also approaching 5000 citations, laid the foundation for a host of subsequent effective-field-theory applications.

Research

Deep Learning Enables Precision Physics on Coarse Spacetime Lattices

Simulating the fundamental forces of nature requires discretizing spacetime into a lattice, but this "pixelation" introduces artifacts that can distort physical results. While renormalization-group theory provides a framework for improved actions that significantly suppress these artifacts, such actions are notoriously difficult to parameterize for practical simulations. In a new Physical Review Letter, Urs Wenger and collaborators bridge the gap between machine learning and quantum field theory by using gauge-equivariant convolutional neural networks to efficiently describe these complex improved actions for SU(3) gauge theory. The researchers demonstrate that this approach achieves a "classically perfect" gradient flow free of leading-order discretization effects, enabling the extraction of continuum properties with high precision even on very coarse lattices. By reducing discretization errors to below 1%, this work establishes machine learning as a transformative tool for overcoming computational barriers in lattice gauge theory.

Awards

2025 High-Energy Physics Prize awarded to Jürg Gasser, Heinrich Leutwyler and Martin Lüscher

We are proud to announce that the 2025 High-Energy Physics Prize of the European Physical Society has been awarded to Jürg Gasser, Heinrich Leutwyler and Martin Lüscher for ground-breaking contributions to new theoretical methods that have advanced our understanding of strong interactions in their non-perturbative regime. In particular, to Gasser and Leutwyler for the formulation of low-energy effective field theory, which opened the way to accurate predictions in hadron physics and in the standard model; to Lüscher for leading developments on the formulation of QCD on the lattice, which enabled the rigorous calculation of non-perturbative contributions to physical observables from first principles.
