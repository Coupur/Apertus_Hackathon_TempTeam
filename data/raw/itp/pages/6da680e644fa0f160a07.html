Soft-Collinear Effective Theory at leading and subleading powers

Starting week 38 (16.09.2025) – every Tuesday from 10am - 12pm in room B1 – ending week 44 (28.10.2025)

Description of the course:

Precise theoretical predictions for scattering processes occurring at colliders are essential for carrying out tests of the Standard Model and searching for physics beyond it. In many cases, the predictions can be systematically improved by calculating terms at higher orders in a perturbative expansion. However, complications arise in regions of phase space where disparate energy scales give rise to large corrections that need to be controlled to all orders in perturbation theory -- for example, the physics associated with soft and collinear emissions of particles in high-energy scattering processes. In these cases, the physical effects associated with degrees of freedom relevant at different mass or distance scales must be disentangled. The Soft-Collinear Effective Field Theory (SCET) is a modern effective field theory designed to analyze these factorization properties and is a convenient framework that enables systematically improvable resummation of enhanced higher order and higher power corrections. These lectures will provide an introduction to basic concepts of SCET, present its applications, and showcase practical approaches to related calculations. The course first explores the physics associated with soft and collinear emissions, where relevant contributions will be identified through the method of regions. Next, I will discuss construction of the SCET Lagrangian and the formalism for describing N-jet processes, which are then used to derive factorization formulas for collider observables at leading and subleading powers. We will work through concrete examples in the case of Drell-Yan production at threshold and the thrust event shape. After obtaining factorization formulas, particular focus will be applied to practical steps in calculating resultant matrix elements and performing resummation through solution of renormalization group equations. The outcome is that the students will learn how this effective field theory can be applied to produce, and improve, theoretical predictions for collider processes with disparate energy scales.

Learning results of the course:

Understand cases where fixed order perturbative approach breaks down and how it has to be reorganised. Develop familiarity with effective field theory tools to approach such problems by considering the relevant degrees of freedom and setting up the effective Lagrangian. Being able to perform necessary calculations within the framework that allow to make all-order statements and regain predictive power of the theory.

Lecturer:

Dr. Sebastian Jaskiewicz
