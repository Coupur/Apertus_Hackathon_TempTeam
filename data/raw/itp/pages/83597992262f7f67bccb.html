Graduate program

Introduction to two-hadron scattering and hadron spectroscopy in Lattice QCD

Starting week 38 (14.09.2026) – every Tuesday from 10am -12pm in room B1 – ending week 44 (30.10.2026)

Description of the course:

While the fundamental theory of the strong interaction, Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD), is well established, predicting the properties of hadrons directly from this theory remains a major challenge. The reason is that, at the energy scales relevant for hadrons, the interaction is so strong that perturbative methods break down. The only systemically controllable method for a non-perturbative treatment of the strong interactions is Lattice QCD. Here, continuous space-time is replaced by a discrete and finite four-dimensional Euclidean lattice. The finite volume has important consequences for the quantities that can be directly computed. In an infinite volume, particles can carry any momentum, and scattering processes lead to a continuous spectrum of energies. By contrast, in a finite box, only discrete momenta are allowed. As a result, the energy spectrum obtained from lattice simulations is also discrete, even for scattering states. Moreover, the concept of well-separated incoming and outgoing particles, on which standard quantum-mechanical scattering theory is based, does not strictly apply in a finite volume. This means that scattering information cannot be directly extracted from Lattice QCD simulations. Instead, one obtains a set of discrete energy levels, from which the desired information must be inferred indirectly. The connection between these finite-volume energy levels and the physical, infinite-volume scattering amplitude is provided by the finite-volume formalism.

In these lectures, we will start by reviewing the basics of Lattice QCD and modern techniques for the calculation of the finite-volume spectrum. Next, we will discuss scattering theory in an infinite volume. Particular attention will be paid to the principles of unitarity, analyticity, and crossing symmetry, as well as to the analytic structure of two-body scattering amplitudes. Afterwards, we will introduce the two-body finite-volume formalism. After motivating the general idea of the formalism, we will derive the corresponding quantisation condition using the skeleton expansion. Finally, we will discuss the practical application of this formalism for constraining infinite- volume two-hadron scattering amplitudes from the Lattice QCD data.

Learning results of the course:

By the end of this course, the participants will be familiar with the basics of methods for calculating the finite- volume spectrum in Lattice QCD. They will also understand the theory behind the two-body finite-volume formalism and be able to apply it to extract constraints on infinite-volume scattering amplitudes.

Lecturer:

Dr. Miguel Salg
