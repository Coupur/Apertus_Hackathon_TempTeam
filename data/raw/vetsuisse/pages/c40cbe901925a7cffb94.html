Research Project

Milk quality premiums and antimicrobial use in dairy cows

The researchers aim to reveal whether milk quality premiums drive antimicrobial use in Swiss dairy herds and how reliably the bulk tank somatic cell count reflects true udder health at farm level.

Nicole Klopfenstein, Beat Thomann

Research Project

Improved delivery of light-activated drugs for skin cancer

Porphyrin-based drugs are promising for photodynamic skin cancer therapy, but suitable delivery systems are lacking. For this, novel carriers and 3D in vitro skin cancer models are developed to improve treatment and reduce animal experiments.

Sabine Kässmeyer, Viola Schmid

Research Project

Swiss Vettriage System: AI-Powered Initial Assessment for Veterinary Emergencies

Swiss Vettriage System aims to develop a scientific base to assess clinical signs and recommend animal owners on their urgence, thereby supporting veterinarians to optimize emergency resources, and advance digitalization in veterinary medicine.

F. Wieland, E. Van der Vekens, E. Studer, N. Fouché, K. Adamik, G. Schüpbach

Research Project

Understanding and Treating Feline Infectious Peritonitis (FIP)

The FIP Projekt Bern is a collaboration between the Small Animal Clinic and the Institute of Virology and Immunology that focuses on characterizing feline coronavirus strains while providing antiviral treatment to cats with FIP in Switzerland.

Alejandra Carranza Valencia, Simone Schuller, Volker Thiel

Research Project

BTV-3 Outbreak: impact on livestock and its price tag

A new bluetongue virus hit Switzerland like a wave. Cattle and sheep are ill, farmers face financial pressure and needed control measures add costs to the national burden. The project estimates the outbreak’s impact and costs to guide control.

Beat Thomann

Research Project

How common is fasciolosis in Lake Chad’s small ruminants?

Liver flukes cause fasciolosis, a disease that harms small ruminants and threatens the livelihoods of nomadic families. This project estimates its true prevalence around Lake Chad and identifies reliable tests to improve surveillance and control.

Dima Farra, Salome Dürr, Valerie Hungerbühler

Research Project

Inter-piglet-intervals as a indicator of dystocia in sows

It is hypothesize that the current inter-piglet-interval threshold for diagnosing dystocia may not reflect free farrowing sow (patho)physiology. The study evaluates links between inter-piglet-intervals, farrowing outcome and sow-related factors.

Philipp T. Egli, Alexander Grahofer

Research Project

Guided Precision for Tiny Bones in Feline Surgery

This project evaluates how computer-guided navigation can improve accuracy and safety when stabilising pelvic injuries in cats to better protect the spinal cord, nearby nerves and other vital structures.

Pia Düver

Research Project

​​Impact of glucose level on the farrowing process in sows​

Maintaining adequate blood glucose can support the farrowing process in crated sows. This study aimed to evaluate the impact of blood glucose levels on sow traits, farrowing kinetics, and piglet parameters under free farrowing conditions.

Lukas Schulthess, Philipp T. Egli, Alexander Grahofer

Research Project

​​Influence of phytotherapy on the puerperium in sows​

Farrowing duration in sows is increasing, raising the risk of puerperal disorders. This study evaluated the effect of intrauterine phytotherapeutic treatment on clinical parameters during the puerperium and on subsequent litter performance.

Philipp T. Egli, Alexander Grahofer

Research Project

Impact of oral NSAID use on sow puerperium

The incidence of postpartum disorders in sows is rising with prolonged farrowing. This study evaluates the effect of oral ketoprofen on the health of sows and their piglets during the lactational period.

Francesca Grech, Alexander Grahofer

Research Project

Phages driving bacterial adaptation and antibiotic resistance

The research group PHAR-OH explores the biology of phage–bacteria interactions to understand how phages shape adaptation and antibiotic resistance in staphylococci across One Health, and leverages this knowledge to advance phage-based solutions for public health.

Elena Gómez-Sanz

Research Project

Brain Under 5G: Development, Function, and Health

The project explores whether 5G-related non-ionizing radiation can influence the development of neurons in young people. Using human brain organoids, the researchers aim to uncover changes in brain structure and function that may affect long-term neuronal health and lead to neurodegeneration.

Meike Mevissen, Angélique Ducray, Rouaa Ben Chaabene, Sélina Thomas, Alex Baumgartner, Daria Tschudin

Research Project

A closer look at a protein behind early Neurodegeneration

At Vetsuisse Faculty, the researchers investigate how NogoA affects the development of neurons linked to Parkinson’s disease. Their work may reveal mechanisms that enable early neuroprotective strategies for Parkinson’s disease and support targeted central nervous system therapies.

Meike Mevissen, Rouaa Ben Chaabene, Angélique Ducray, Michaela Waeber, Sara Alonso, Léa Walther

Research Project

Artificial Intelligence-Based Assessment of Nostril Stenosis in Brachycephalic Dogs

The project develops an AI-driven system for objective assessment of nostril stenosis in brachycephalic dogs, improving diagnostic consistency, informing evidence-based surgical decisions, and strengthening standardized evaluations in breeding programs

Deborah Rohner

Research Project

Augmented Anatomy: User Feedback-Driven Teaching Units

To provide flexible anatomy study based on defined learning aims, augmented reality teaching units with realistic animal models are developed through continuous user feedback. Learning progress is tracked by self-tests.

Sabine Kaessmeyer, Barbara Drews

Research Project

Unlocking the Genetics of South American Camelids

Alpacas and llamas show diverse fleece types and colors. The livestock genetics team with international partners studies genome data to uncover genetic variants driving inherited traits and rare diseases like deafness in blue-eyed white animals.

Anna Letko, Cord Drögemüller

Research Project

Tracking Tails – An Interdisciplinary Approach to Understanding Cultural and Environmental Factors in Canine Disease Transmission and Control

The project aims to generate context-specific insights into how human-influenced dog movement drives disease transmission, enabling researchers to develop locally adapted models that support effective and culturally acceptable control strategies.

Ayrin Piephoh, Silja Griss, Jolanda Paganoni, Salome Dürr

Research Project

Optimizing FRD population and movement estimates

Closing gaps in dog ecology is key to better rabies control, including mass dog vaccination. This project will define the demographics of India’s free-roaming dogs, estimate populations with varied methods, and test a simpler survey-based HR tool.

Laura Cunha Silva, Salome Dürr

Res

Optimized barn design and management for healthier calves

Straw-bedded outdoor areas for veal calves have been shown to be associated with better health (lower antibiotic use and death rate) than unsheltered areas under experimental conditions. This concept is now tested under “real life” conditions.

Jens Becker, Mireille Meylan, Philipp Schnydrig

Research Project

Sustainable improvement of bovine health using genomic data

The Vetsuisse livestock genetics team is working with experts at Qualitas Zug and ETH Zurich to identify harmful gene variants and develop selection tools that will prevent inherited disorders and improve health and welfare of Swiss cattle.

Joana Jacinto, Cord Drögemüller

Research Project

Characterizing influenza A viruses in humans & pigs

To assess the potential for zoonotic & anthroponotic transmission of IAVs, samples from humans having pig contact & from pigs will be collected. Positives will be subjected to whole genome amplification & sequencing to enable characterization.

Jonas Steiner, Heiko Nathues

Research Project

Detection of Salmonella in Swiss Pig Herds

A nationwide study found Salmonella in only 0.4% of 1,143 fecal samples from Swiss pigs. While confirming a low prevalence compared to neighboring countries, the results highlight the need for monitoring to protect food safety and animal health.

Nico Christiner, Heiko Nathues

Research Project

Impact of miniaturisation of dogs on their brain

Spitz dogs of all sizes undergo MRI to investigate cerebrospinal fluid flow. Flow disturbances are linked to hydrocephalus and syringomyelia. The Vetsuisse Faculty establishes evidence-based breeding criteria for a healthier dog population.

Daniela Schweizer, Sarah Hubler

Research Project

HenData: A comprehensive profile to improve poultry welfare

In order to breed laying hens that are more resilient, the project uses tracking technology within a commercial barn to identify how movement of laying hen influences and is affected by disease and injury, genetics, and neurogenesis.

Michael Toscano, Matthew Petelle, Sabine Gebhardt, Alex Johny

Research Project

Assessment of knowledge, attitudes and practices (KAP) towards rabies

This project reviews KAP studies on dog-mediated rabies, develops a validated KAP questionnaire, and applies it in a Kenyan county where rabies control programs are underway to identify gaps that hinder effective control.

Filipe Maximiano Sousa, Salome Dürr

Research Project

What is a healthy dairy cow?

Livestock health is often seen solely as the absence of disease. At Vetsuisse, researchers go further, using AI and stakeholder input to build a comprehensive Determinants of Health model for dairy cattle.

Luís Pedro Carmo

Research Project

eRabies - towards rabies elimination in Uganda

Canine rabies elimination is possible. For this, at least 70% of the dog must be vaccinated; and it is important that dog bite cases and rabies cases are reported. The eRabies project investigates these and other aspects – on the towards rabies elimination in Uganda.

Salome Dürr

Research Project

Participation in the National Foot Rot Control Program

As part of the national foot rot control program, the ambulatory division of the Ruminant Clinic tests and oversees approximately 130 sheep farms in the Region of Bern. In collaboration with the Veterinary Office of the Canton of Bern, data related to the control efforts are being collected and incorporated into a research project.

Isabelle Rediger, Eveline Studer

Research Project

Antimicrobial resistance in the fecal flora of dairy cows

Resistance testing of E. coli isolated from fecal swab samples from sick cows with or without previous treatment and from healthy control cows will investigate resistance patterns to important antimicrobials of highest priority in human medicine

Mireille Meylan, Vincent Perreten, Bettina Küng

Research Project

What causes radio- and chemotherapy resistance in cancer?

The Rottenberg group aims to understand mechanisms of radio- and chemotherapy resistance in cancer in humans and animals. They search for approaches to overcome resistance, which is the main cause of death of patients with metastasized tumors.

Sven Rottenberg

01-20

Research Project

Does inbreeding compromise stress resilience in lab mice?

This study aims to answer whether inbred mice are less robust and less resilient to stress compared to heterozygous mice, and thus whether inbreeding compromises the animals’ health and well-being and the reproducibility of research findings.

Bernhard Völkl, Larisa Kaija, Pui Ching Chu, Hanno Würbel

Research Project

Drug resistance & new treatments against liver flukes

The common liver fluke causes liver disease in humans and animals. As common medications are threatened by the development of drug resistance, we study the molecular mechanisms behind resistance and new active molecules against the liver fluke.

Natalie Wiedemar

Research Project

Prevention and treatment of toxoplasmosis and neosporosis

Development of antiparasitic drug treatments for neosporosis, toxoplasmosis and related diseases caused by apicomplexan parasites, and vaccines against neosporosis in cattle, caused by Neospora caninum.

Andrew Hemphill

Research Project

Development of novel treatments for fox tapeworm infection

Alveolar echinococcosis is a life-threatening infection caused by the fox tapeworm. Since current drugs only inhibit parasite growth, we develop new therapies targeting key metabolic pathways to achieve more effective, curative treatments.

Britta Lundström

Research Project

Decoding donkey endocrinopathies for better care

The researchers investigate clinical features of endocrinopathies in donkeys and seek to enhance laboratory diagnosis through hormone analysis. They address welfare concerns, as the associated laminitis can be painful and may require euthanasia.

Solange Oesch

Research Project

Zebrafish domestication

The project aims to draw conclusions on domestication processes and genetic adaptation to «life as a lab pet» in zebrafish.

Irene Adrian-Kalchhauser

Research Project

The brown trout immune system

The SNF-funded project characterizes the effect of rearing environment on adult immune responses of fish towards a parasite.

Irene Adrian-Kalchhauser

Research Project

Aquaculture Microbiomes

The SNF-funded project investigates which characteristics of microbiomes in recirculating aquaculture are linked to health and disease of fish.

Irene Adrian-Kalchhauser

Research Project

The feeding of antimicrobial residue-laden milk to calves

The project’s goal is to document the exposure of calves on Swiss dairy farms to antimicrobials in waste milk, and to evaluate the association between such exposure and the carriage of antimicrobial-resistant bacteria in calves.

Véronique Bernier Gosselin, Mireille Meylan

Research Project

​​Neuromonitoring in Trigeminal-Mediated Headshaking (TMHS)​

TMHS is a poorly understood equine disease. The researchers seek to create non-invasive tools to assess trigeminal nerve function and establish intraoperative neuromonitoring methods for targeted trigeminal ganglion rhizotomy.

M.Käfer-Karrer, C.Spadavecchia, M.de Preux, L.Unger, C.Koch

Research Project

The Role of Minnows as Intermediate Hosts in the Life Cycle of Tetracapsuloides bryosalmonae

Proliferative kidney disease (PKD), caused by the parasite Tetracapsuloides bryosalmonae, is a notifiable condition that affects endangered wild brown trout. We systematically examine the parasite’s life cycle and the role of non-salmonid species as intermediate and reservoir hosts. Our findings hold important implications for epidemiology and management.

Céline Möckli, Nicolas Diserens, Heike Schmidt-Posthaus

Research Project

DROP: Effects of Different temperature Regimes on host-Pathogen dynamics in freshwater ecosystems

Host-pathogen dynamics in freshwater ecosystems are influenced by environmental temperature, which affects the host's fitness as well as pathogen's growth and virulence. This international, SNF funded study investigates how realistic current and projected temperature regimes affect the dynamics of crayfish plague, a disease in endangered crayfish species.

Eliane Jemmi, Simone Pisano, Heike Schmidt-Posthaus

Research Project

When alpacas help understand asthma in horses

The Vetsuisse Faculty aims to advance the understanding of equine asthma by developing a novel diagnostic test based on alpaca-derived nanobodies targeting CXCL13, a promising biomarker for this frequent respiratory disease in horses.

Anna Molnár, Sophie Sage, Eliane Marti, Philippe Plattet, Morgane Subran-Di Palma, Marco Alves, Vinzenz Gerber

Research Project

Study on canine mast cell tumors on tumor targeting

The researchers integrate near-infrared fluorescence imaging with indocyanine green-loaded tumor-derived nanoparticles to enhance the precision of tumor and lymph node removal in dogs, aiming to reduce recurrence and advance surgical oncology.

Simona Vincenti, Marilena de Matteo, Sara Soto

Research Project

EV proteomics to detect residual cancer in dogs

The researchers use proteomic profiling of extracellular vesicles from dogs with mast cell tumors to identify biomarkers of minimal residual disease and recurrence, aiming to advance liquid biopsy approaches in veterinary oncology.

Simona Vincenti, Marilena de Matteo, Manfred Heller, Sophie Braga, Anne-Christine Uldry

Research Project

Can pain inhibit pain in horses – and can AI detect it?

To better understand conditioned pain modulation - how pain can inhibit pain - in horses, researchers at the Vetsuisse Faculty study this mechanism with AI support, aiming to improve pain assessment, management, and animal welfare.

Claudia Spadavecchia

Research Project

Bridging Clinic and Pathogen: development of a precise and practical antemortem BRD Diagnosis

The overarching aim of this project will be to advance the understanding of BRD in dairy pre-weaning calves by integrating high-resolution clinical assessment and pathogen profiling. By doing so, it will improve animal health, welfare, and sustainability, aligning with One Health and Green Deal.

Claire Lefèvre, Jacinto Joana, Zanolari Patrik, Bodmer Michèle

Research Project

Development of a genetic test for PRA in Saarloos wolfdogs

Progressive retinal atrophy (PRA) is an inherited disease leading to complete blindness. Researchers at the Vetsuisse Faculty investigate the causal genetic defect to enable genetic testing and avoiding the breeding of further affected dogs.

Tosso Leeb

Research Project

Clinical research in prognosis, management, and potential treatment options for traumatic spinal cord injuries

Traumatic spinal cord injuries pose a major challenge in veterinary neurology and can cause severe motor deficits to plegia. The Division of Clinical Neurology aims to develop innovative therapies to improve outcomes.

Veronika Stein, Enrice Hünerfauth, Nicolas Schott

Forschungsprojekt

How does milk composition affect neonatal thermoregulation?

Optimal development and successful metabolic adaptation are essential for health of neonatal farm animals. The researchers of the Vetsuisse Faculty are developing new feeding strategies to optimize calf development in different environmental conditions.

Josef J. Gross
