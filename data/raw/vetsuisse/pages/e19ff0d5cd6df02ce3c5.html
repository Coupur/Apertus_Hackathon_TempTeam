Advancing knowledge together for animals, people, and the planet

Our outstanding achievements in teaching and research position us as one of Europe’s leading veterinary faculties. The knowledge cultivated in Bern finds practical application in the services and training programs offered by our veterinary clinics and institutes.
We are passionately committed to preserving and promoting animal welfare and health. In partnership with related disciplines, we contribute to sustainable development and the well-being of both humans and animals within a healthy environment.

EAEVE: Vetsuisse Accredited Without Reservation

At its meeting on September 28, the European Committee of Veterinary Education (ECOVE) once again granted unconditional accreditation to the Vetsuisse Faculty, with its campuses in Bern and Zurich, through the European Association of Establishments for Veterinary Education (EAEVE). The accreditation had been suspended in the fall of 2024 after the EAEVE identified deficiencies. These deficiencies pertained in particular to training in food hygiene and biosafety at both campuses. These deficiencies have been fully resolved—veterinary education in Switzerland now meets all European standards.

Top ranking for Vetsuisse Faculty again

The Vetsuisse Faculty with its locations in Bern and Zurich is considered the seventh best veterinary faculty in the world. Excellent research and a good reputation for teaching have contributed to this ranking. The QS World University Ranking analyzed 418 veterinary departments - the Vetsuisse Faculty is ranked in the top ten for the sixth time in a row.

Featured Projects

Research Project

Drug resistance & new treatments against liver flukes

The common liver fluke causes liver disease in humans and animals. As common medications are threatened by the development of drug resistance, we study the molecular mechanisms behind resistance and new active molecules against the liver fluke.

Natalie Wiedemar

Research Project

Bridging Clinic and Pathogen: development of a precise and practical antemortem BRD Diagnosis

The overarching aim of this project will be to advance the understanding of BRD in dairy pre-weaning calves by integrating high-resolution clinical assessment and pathogen profiling. By doing so, it will improve animal health, welfare, and sustainability, aligning with One Health and Green Deal.

Claire Lefèvre, Jacinto Joana, Zanolari Patrik, Bodmer Michèle

Research Project

Development of novel treatments for fox tapeworm infection

Alveolar echinococcosis is a life-threatening infection caused by the fox tapeworm. Since current drugs only inhibit parasite growth, we develop new therapies targeting key metabolic pathways to achieve more effective, curative treatments.

Britta Lundström

Research Project

Milk quality premiums and antimicrobial use in dairy cows

The researchers aim to reveal whether milk quality premiums drive antimicrobial use in Swiss dairy herds and how reliably the bulk tank somatic cell count reflects true udder health at farm level.

Nicole Klopfenstein, Beat Thomann
