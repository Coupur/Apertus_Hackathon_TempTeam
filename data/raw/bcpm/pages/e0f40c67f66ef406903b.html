The Bern Center for Precision Medicine (BCPM) and its members create a robust precision medicine approach to treating patients in health and disease:

  • The center was officially founded on May 20, 2019.
  • Seven pilot projects were selected for funding from the 1st pilot project call, eight pilot projects from the 2nd project call and six winning projects from the Covid-19/SARS-CoV-2 project call.
  • 5 winning projects for Young Investigators were announced in November 2021.
  • 6 winning projects in Cancer Research were selected and announced in June 2022.
  • 1 winning project in Ethics and Data Exchange was selected in October 2022.
  • 1 winning Lighthouse project was selected in July 2023.
  • Currently, more than 100 members have joined the center, among them important precision medicine platforms such as genomics, biobank, etc.

News and Events

News

Closure of the Bern Center for Precision Medicine

Following a decision taken by the Executive Board of the University of Bern, the Bern Center for Precision Medicine (BCPM) will be closed on December 31, 2026.

We would like to thank the BCPM members for their valuable and active contributions – which lead to many positive results and the establishment of Precision Medicine in Bern. The final activity report can be found in the "About us" section above.

Publications

Going beyond genomics in precision oncology

A large population-based study from Japan reveals both the promise and the limits of genomic profiling in cancer care, and highlights the need for multidimensional data to guide therapy across diverse populations.

Research News

Novel gene therapy for life-threatening cardiac arrhythmia

Coming out of the BCPM and its PACE lighthouse project, researchers at the Bern University Hospital and the University of Bern, and in collaboration with the Mayo Clinic, Rochester, USA, have developed and tested a novel gene therapy that successfully corrects a lifethreatening genetic cardiac arrhythmia in an animal model.

Symposium

International Conference on Precision Cardiology

In autumn 2025, the BCPM and the PACE Lighthouse Consortium held their symposium, featuring international experts in fields such as ion channels, precision cardiology and precision medicine in general. A special focus was given on the latest advancements in research on the Long QT Syndrome (LQTS). Presenters were Katja Odening and Nadia Mercader from Bern, Milena Bellin from Padua, José Jalife from Madrid and Lia Crotti from Milano. There were some 50 guests, among them renowned researchers in the field.
