African universities are important partners for the University of Bern. With the Initiative Afrique, the UniBE is placing a new emphasis on its internationalisation strategy, which brings together expertise in Africa and promotes networking. On this website, you will find information on existing activities and resources.

For many years, the University of Bern has been strongly committed to promoting science and fostering global research networks, particularly with partners in Africa. "We firmly believe that tackling global problems requires global cooperation – a principle on which our internationalisation strategy is based," says Hugues Abriel, Vice-Rector for Research and Innovation at the University of Bern.

The Initiative Afrique was created by the Vice-Rectorate Research and Innovation and the Vice-Rectorate International and Academic Careers. It serves as a platform for bundling all the research activities related to Africa and facilitating better networking within and outside the University of Bern.

African Perspectives on Global Transformations

From August 26 to 28, 2026, Initiative Afrique participated in the conference “African Perspectives on Global Transformations,” co-organized by the SSAS and the VAD e.V. at the University of Basel. The conference brought together 448 in-person participants and 277 online participants from Africa and Europe.

News

Science Diplomacy in Practice

On Monday, 17 of August 2026, the Africa Division at the State Secretariat of the FDFA, Initiative Afrique at the University of Bern, and the Swiss Leading House Africa at the University of Basel co-organized the strategic workshop on Science Diplomacy in Practice, convening Swiss and African diplomats, academics, and institutional partners.

News

Initiative Afrique in Africa-Europe International Collaboration

International research collaboration is increasingly shifting towards more equitable and interconnected models, as research organisations across regions recognise the need to move beyond traditional bilateral approaches. Growing emphasis is being placed on reciprocity, shared priorities, and co-creation in global research partnerships.
