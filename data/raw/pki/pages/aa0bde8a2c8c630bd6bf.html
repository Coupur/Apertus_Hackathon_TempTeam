September 2026: Publication Bourquin Research Group

Targeting the gasotransmitter H2S to boost the antitumor immune response in renal cancer

Prof. Bourquin’s team at the Institute of Pharmacology, in collaboration with researchers from the University of Fribourg, has identified a new target to improve cancer treatment. Recently published in Redox Biology, the study shows that high expression of the enzyme 3-mercaptopyruvate sulfurtransferase (3-MST) — a key enzyme in the endogenous production of hydrogen sulfide (H₂S) — is associated with reduced survival in patients with renal cancer. The team demonstrated that pharmacological inhibition of 3-MST decreases H2S production in renal cancer cells, disrupts cell metabolism, and induces apoptosis. Importantly, 3-MST inhibition also enhances the ability of CD8+ T cells to kill cancer cells, an effect further amplified by PD-L1 blockade. Together, these results suggest that targeting the 3-MST/H2S pathway represents a promising therapeutic strategy to enhance the efficacy of cancer immunotherapy in renal cancer. Read the full article: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.redox.2026.104373.

August 2026: Kick-off research project

Modelling of lung lymphatic system on-chip

Prof. Carole Bourquin, Prof. Olivier Guenat (ARTORG Center, University of Bern) and Prof. Cornelia Halin (ETH Zurich) have jointly initiated a new collaborative project, which was recently launched with a kick-off meeting. This tripartite initiative will investigate the role of the lung lymphatic system in healthy and disease state. While the vascular and lymphatic systems are known to be essential for lung homeostasis, fluid balance, immune surveillance, and tissue repair, it remains poorly understood, how morphological and functional changes in these systems contribute to lung pathologies. To address this gap, the team will recreate the lung lymphatic system on-chip, modelling human blood and lymphatic vessels in a dynamic 3D microfluidic environment. Drawing on the combined expertise of the three research groups, spanning immunopharmacology, bioengineering and vascular biology, the resulting platform will enable researchers to study vascular-lymphatic interactions, lymphatic drainage, and immune-cell trafficking. The ultimate goal of this project is to develop and characterize a human-relevant model to study the underlying mechanisms and functional consequences of lymphatic dysfunction in lung diseases such as chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), with future potential for pharmacological testing of new therapeutic approaches.

August 2026: Article von Gunten Group

Treating Appendicitis Without Surgery?

The research group led by Prof. von Gunten at the Institute of Pharmacology has identified a new pharmacological strategy for treating appendicitis and other so-called neutrophil-dominant diseases. This strategy could help avoid stressful surgical procedures, particularly in children. Laboratory results show that the anti-inflammatory effects of human antibody preparations are more effective at eliminating neutrophil inflammatory cells than steroid preparations. These antibody preparations could complement and significantly improve current non-surgical strategies involving antibiotics. This study was conducted in collaboration with the Inselspital and could inspire further clinical trials.

Publication Pharmacology

July 2026: Publication in Cell Death & Differentiation

Hitting two survival switches at once kills lung cancer cells within hours

Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is notoriously hard to kill: the cells survive chemotherapy because proteins of the BCL-2 family keep their built-in self-destruct programme, apoptosis, switched off. Liyang Wu and colleagues from the group of Thomas Kaufmann screened ten human NSCLC cell lines with drugs that block these survival proteins one at a time — and found that blocking any single one has little effect. Blocking two of them together, BCL-XL and MCL-1, was a different story: most of the cell lines committed to apoptosis within a few hours, and the effect was irreversible, independent of the frequently mutated tumour suppressor p53, and strictly dependent on the mitochondrial cell death machinery. Cell lines that initially looked resistant were in fact simply not fully hit, and died once BCL-XL was blocked more effectively or removed altogether using the PROTAC degrader DT2216 — a platelet-sparing approach that addresses the main side effect that has held BCL-XL inhibitors back in the clinic. Together, the study reframes apoptosis resistance in NSCLC as a joint effort of two proteins rather than a dependency on one, and points to a fast, potentially exploitable therapeutic vulnerability. Figure from Wu et al., Cell Death & Differentiation 2026 shows: one week after treatment: lung cancer cells survive each drug on its own (dishes 2+3, purple colonies), but combined inhibition of BCL-XL (A-1331852) and MCL-1 (S63845) leaves no survivors.

April 2026: Publication in Cell Reports

Targeting PI3Kδ suppresses pancreatic cancer

We sincerely congratulate Georgia Konstantinidou and her team for the recent publication "Targeting PI3Kδ suppresses pancreatic cancer by dual disruption of fibrosis and immune evasion" in the journal "Cell Reports". In this study, the autors identify PI3Kδ as a key regulator of tumor-stroma-immune crosstalk in PDAC (Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma). Its inhibition simultaneously reduces fibrosis and alleviates immune suppression, leading to decreased tumor progression and metastasis. These findings highlight PI3Kδ as a promising therapeutic target to overcome key barriers in pancreatic cancer. DOI: 10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117188 or please follow link below:

January 2026: When art meets science

Visualizing cancer research

A recent study led by Prof. Bourquin was featured in the Art and Science project jointly organized by the Faculty of Science at the University of Geneva and the Geneva School of Art and Design (HEAD). This initiative aims to translate complex scientific concepts into a visually compelling image that can engage diverse audiences. As part of this collaboration, Eloise Dupuychaffray, then a PhD student in Prof. Bourquin’s laboratory, presented her research to HEAD students, who were invited to propose original artistic interpretations. The scientific study shows that although obesity is a major risk factor for cancer, it may paradoxically enhance the efficacy of cancer immunotherapy in males with melanoma. Using in vitro models and clinical data, the researchers demonstrate that increased estrogen production in the adipose tissue of obese males is associated with an improved response to anti-cancer immunotherapy. To illustrate these findings, Sophie Hache, a HEAD student, created a narrative drawing that guides the viewer from estrogen production in adipose tissue to immune-cell activation and the destruction of cancer cells. This illustration accompanies a lay summary of the study published in "The Science Breaker", making the research accessible to a broad public audience.

October 2025: New SNF Grant

Prof. Carole Bourquin - New SNF Grant backs innovative organ-on-a-chip approach to Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease

We are pleased to announce the award of a new Swiss National Science Foundation (SNF) Project Grant to support a collaborative research project between Prof. Carole Bourquin (Institute of Pharmacology), Prof. Olivier Guenat (ARTORG Center, University of Bern), and Prof. Cornelia Halin (ETH Zurich). This project aims to advance our understanding of the human lung’s lymphatic vasculature in both healthy and diseased states, with a particular focus on chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). By integrating advanced imaging, pharmacological approaches, and innovative microfluidic “organ-on-chip” technology, the interdisciplinary team will investigate lymphatic morphology, function, and immune cell interactions under physiological and pathological conditions. The results are expected to provide new insights into the inflammatory processes driving COPD progression and to pave the way for the development of targeted pharmacological strategies to restore lymphatic function and mitigate disease severity.

October 2025: Research award

von Gunten research team - Winner of Novartis FreeNovation 2025

Prof. Stephan von Gunten and junior researcher Lukas Mürner from the Institute of Pharmacology at the University of Bern are being awarded for innovative research, receiving research funding by the Novartis Research Foundation's FreeNovation 2025-Program. FreeNovation is intended to encourage unconventional thinking and to further strengthen Switzerland's research position. The funded research project focuses on complex sugar structures (Glycans) on the surface of pancreatic cancer cells, playing a key role in central biological processes such as tumor growth, metastatic spread and evasion of immunological defence mechanisms. Despite their promising diagnostic and therapeutic potential, these structurally diverse carbohydrates have so far eluded broad analysis using currently available methods. By using state-of-the-art technologies in combination with innovative screening strategies and novel methods for the production of glycan specific reagents, a more profound access to the functional and biological significance of glycane structures is to be made possible. This until today unachieved analytical depth not only promises an expanded understanding of the molecular properties of pancreatic tumors, but also the development of new diagnostic standards as well as the identification of potential therapeutic targets.
