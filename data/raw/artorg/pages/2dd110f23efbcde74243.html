ARTORG’s AI in Health and Nutrition Group Contributes AI Expertise to Horizon Europe Project AIRIS

AIRIS (Mechanism-Informed Multimodal Generative AI for Causal and Dynamical Modelling in Biomedical Research) brings together 21 partners from Europe, Canada, and the US to develop generative AI models that combine biomedical and clinical data, existing knowledge, and mechanistic models. ARTORG researchers will contribute expertise in causal AI, mechanism discovery, and multimodal modelling to advance AI-supported biomedical discovery and personalized medicine.

Patients with the same diagnosis can experience very different disease courses and treatment outcomes, shaped by a complex interplay of biological and environmental factors. However, current generative AI systems aimed at modelling such diseases primarily learn statistical correlations from their training data rather than the underlying biological mechanisms that cause the disease. This challenge is particularly pronounced in complex diseases whose underlying mechanisms remain only partially understood.

To address this fundamental challenge, AIRIS (Mechanism-Informed Multimodal Generative AI for Causal and Dynamical Modelling in Biomedical Research) brings together the multidisciplinary expertise of 21 research and industry partners from nine European countries, the United States, and Canada. Over the next four years, the consortium will receive €16.9 million in funding from the European Union’s Horizon Europe Programme to develop a generative AI platform that builds and reasons with mechanistic models of disease, rather than relying solely on statistical patterns. The goal is to design a platform that assists researchers in identifying previously unknown disease pathways and developing novel scientific hypotheses.

The University of Bern contributes to AIRIS through the Artificial Intelligence in Health and Nutrition (AIHN) research group at the ARTORG Center for Biomedical Engineering Research, led by Prof. Dr. Stavroula Georgia Mougiakakou, and the Department of Health Psychology and Behavioral Medicine at the Institute of Psychology, led by Prof. Dr. Jennifer Inauen. The ARTORG team will play a key role in developing methods to formalize and discover disease mechanisms from biomedical knowledge and multimodal data, including causal and dynamical modelling approaches, and in connecting these mechanisms with generative AI. Pulmonary fibrosis will serve as one of the project's five major biomedical use cases, supported by close collaboration with the University Institute of Diagnostic, Interventional and Pediatric Radiology at Inselspital, Bern University Hospital, and other clinical partners.

In AIRIS, we aim to combine the capabilities of AI with established scientific knowledge and mechanistic models from biology and medicine to extend our ability to understand complex diseases beyond what these approaches can achieve independently. By bringing these strengths together, we seek to uncover underlying disease mechanisms, accelerate scientific discovery, and ultimately contribute to better and more effective treatments.
— Prof. Dr. Stavroula Georgia Mougiakakou

The AIRIS project is coordinated by Prof. Christos Diou, Associate Professor at the Athena Research Center in Greece, and officially began on June 1, 2026. The full press release and future project updates will be available on the AIRIS Project website. More information on the project is also available on the European Commission CORDIS website.
