Two ARTORG Research Groups receive BRIDGE Proof of Concept Grant

The ARTORG Center’s Gerontechnology and Rehabilitation (GER) and Organs-on-Chip Technologies (OOC) groups each receive a BRIDGE Proof of Concept grant, securing CHF 130,000 in funding for a researcher’s project for the coming year.

The BRIDGE Proof of Concept Grant is a financing scheme offered by the BRIDGE Funding Programme, a joint effort by the Swiss National Science Foundation (SNSF) and Innosuisse, the Swiss Innovation Agency. This grant offers young researchers recently or soon finished with their PhD the opportunity to develop their idea into a social or commercial innovation.

In the December 2025 call, 17 projects were selected for funding, among them two associated with the ARTORG Center: Nic Krummenacher, a PhD student with the Gerontechnology and Rehabilitation group (GER), and Dr. Soheila Zeinali, a senior scientist with the Organs-on-Chip Technologies group (OOC). With this grant, they each receive funding for the next 12 months to further develop and advance their innovative project.

The ARTORG Center warmly congratulates Nic and Soheila on this amazing achievement and wishes them great success with their projects!

Details on the two projects:

Nic Krummenacher

Advancing Telerehabilitation Capabilities with DextEgg Finger Dexterity Training

Nic Krummenacher (GER) was awarded CHF 129,953.

Fine motor impairments of the hand, affecting grip, finger coordination, and dexterity, significantly reduce independence in daily life for over 200 million people worldwide, including those living with stroke, Parkinson's disease, multiple sclerosis, and age-related decline. While high-dose repetitive training is proven to improve outcomes, a persistent shortage of occupational therapists and extremely low home exercise adherence (5–10%) create a growing "Rehabilitation Gap": six months after stroke, only 30–40% of patients recover functional hand use, compared to 60–80% who regain walking ability.

During his PhD at the ARTORG Center, Nic Krummenacher developed the DextEgg system, a low-cost, sensor-equipped, egg-shaped handgrip paired with a gamified patient app, to address this gap. Clinical validation across multiple populations (stroke, Parkinson's, MS, geriatric) demonstrated 95% adherence to prescribed home training and high user acceptance.

However, DextEgg currently lacks a therapist-facing component for remote professional supervision. This BRIDGE project will develop and validate a web-based therapist dashboard that transforms DextEgg into a scalable "blended care" platform for fine motor rehabilitation. The dashboard will enable therapists to remotely monitor patient adherence, review exercise performance, and adjust training programs between visits, integrating high-dose home training into routine clinical workflows.

The 12-month project, conducted in partnership with VITREA Schweiz, involves iterative co-design of the dashboard with occupational therapists and a clinical feasibility study across multiple VITREA rehabilitation centers.

Dr. Soheila Zeinali

Human vTME Panel: Closing Preclinical Gap in ADC Development

Dr. Soheila Zeinali (OOC) was awarded CHF 130,000.

This project aims to de-risk and accelerate the development of targeted anti-cancer therapies by transforming human vascularized, target-specific tumor models into a contract research organization service. Current preclinical models — including conventional in vitro assays and animal xenografts — fail to adequately predict clinical drug efficacy and toxicity because they lack a functional human tumor microenvironment.

Building on research conducted at the Organs-on-chip Technologies Group at ARTORG Center, we have developed next-generation in vitro tumor models that incorporate human-derived microvasculature and stromal components, enabling the assessment of both on-target and off-target drug effects within a clinically relevant timeframe.

Since the time of application, the spin-off company vitronco AG (www.vitronco.com) has been legally incorporated in Bern, Switzerland. By the end of the project, we expect to deliver a platform that provides clinically translatable drug efficacy, mechanism-of-action, and toxicity profiles — reducing translational risk and time to clinic for targeted anti-cancer therapies, while contributing to reduced animal use in preclinical testing.
