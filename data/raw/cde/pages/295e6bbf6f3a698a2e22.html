Inspiring sustainable futures

CDE is Switzerland’s centre of excellence for sustainable development. One of the University of Bern’s strategic research centres, we are tasked with mainstreaming sustainability throughout the university’s research and teaching. We conduct research and teaching on behalf of a more sustainable world, combining sound research with inter- and transdisciplinary approaches. Our research agenda is created within long-standing partnerships spanning the global North and South.

Tribute to Prof. em. Dr. Dr. h.c. Hans Hurni

It is with deep sadness that we bid farewell to Prof. em. Dr. Dr. h.c. Hans Hurni. The co-founder and long-time Director of the Centre for Development and Environment (CDE) at the University of Bern passed away at the age of 75 on 27 September 2026, following an extended period of illness. A tribute by his colleague and friend, Prof. em. Dr. Urs Wiesmann.

Livestock farming in Tanzania

Population growth and shrinking water and land resources are escalating conflicts between wildlife and human interests across many parts of Africa. One such hotspot is Mlele District in western Tanzania. Located amid several protected areas, it’s a region where some local villagers rely on cattle farming to make a living. What can help to mitigate these human–wildlife conflicts? Visit our new online exhibition and find out more.

SCIENCE4POLICY

The jobs are there. Why aren’t local workers being hired?

For nearly two decades, Laos has promoted international commercial agricultural investments as a core pillar of its national development strategy. Such investments – typically structured via land concessions – were seen as a promising way of creating jobs in rural areas. But these opportunities haven’t materialized as anticipated. A CDE study asks why – and investigates how to minimize the gap between investor and worker expectations. Get more information.

Fostering a climate-resilient landscape in Köniz municipality

How can local landscapes help mitigate climate change while becoming more resilient to its effects? The “Climate Landscape Köniz” project works with land users and the municipality of Köniz, near Bern, to address these questions – both in the field and within local government.

What does sustainability mean for the Swiss economy?

How can a society conduct economic activity without undermining the very foundations of its livelihood? For Switzerland, this question is now more urgent than ever – concerning everything from corporate governance to balancing the budget. According to CDE’s Christoph Bader and Thomas Breu, key questions of sustainability should finally be placed at the centre of Switzerland’s economic and regulatory framework.Read their blog post in our sustainability series.
