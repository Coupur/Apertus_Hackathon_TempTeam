2030 Agenda: Switzerland is lagging behind in implementation

Switzerland is set to fall short of most of the 17 Sustainable Development Goals of the 2030 Agenda. The Federal Council now intends to focus on those goals where “the impact is greatest”. Impact is not achieved simply by concentrating on fewer goals, says CDE Director Sabin Bieri. “For us to take action, we also need a vision that inspires and engages us all.” However, the Federal Council’s approach is a long way from that.
Date: 17 July 2026 | Source: SRF Echo der Zeit (in German)

“We are abandoning the vision of a more just world”

The Federal Council is planning drastic budget reallocations at the expense of development cooperation. CDE Director Sabin Bieri comments: “What is alarming is that something much bigger is happening: we are abandoning the idea that we share a common responsibility and are pursuing the goal of making life better for everyone.”

Mercosur trade agreement: Controversy over environmental protection and sustainability

The Swiss Parliament is expected to ratify the Mercosur free trade agreement this week. The potential environmental consequences are a point of controversy. CDE’s Elisabeth Bürgi Bonanomi has reservations about whether the agreement adequately takes into account the sustainability objectives set out in the Federal Constitution. She also raises concerns about deforestation in South America and about pesticides that are authorised in various Mercosur countries but banned in Switzerland or the EU. Despite her reservations, she doesn’t reject the agreement in principle, but calls for additional measures, similar to those in the EU, to ensure sustainability and prevent the import of products from deforested areas.
Date: 17 June 2026 | Source: SRF Echo der Zeit (in German)

Language of accidents

Linguists from CDE, the University of Vienna, and the Potsdam Geoscience Research Centre have analysed reports of road traffic accidents. The researchers have identified a distinct "accident language" characterised by passive phrasing and the absence of active actors. Their recommendations urge the police and the media not to portray accidents as acts of fate, but as human-made events; to name the people involved and describe their actions; and to clearly set out the perspectives of those involved.
Date: 19 April 2026 | Source: Sonntagsblick (in German)

The future world of work requires courage

Switzerland is one of the European countries with the longest working weeks, but also with the highest proportion of part-time workers. This raises the question: why not reduce working hours overall? “A reduction in working hours could enable a fairer distribution of the remaining work among employees,” says Stephanie Moser, CDE. She advocates thinking further about new models. “We are interested, for example, in whether and how the boundaries between traditional paid work and other types of work could be rethought. After all, we don’t just work for a job, we also do a lot of unpaid work that is very important for society. What might a model look like that better reconciles these two categories?”

Local implementation of climate neutrality

In the Bernese Oberland, 28 municipalities have jointly initiated a discussion process with the aim of taking concrete steps to reduce greenhouse gas emissions in the region. "It is crucial that the process is initiated from within the region and ideally anchored in a joint agreement - for example a strategy paper - that legitimises it," says CDE scientist Stephanie Moser. Other success factors are that the participants in the workshops are selected in a conscious and balanced manner and that the discussion process is open-ended, creates space for creativity and reflects the outcomes.
