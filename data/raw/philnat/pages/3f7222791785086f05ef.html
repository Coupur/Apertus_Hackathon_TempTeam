The Faculty of Science

The Faculty of Science consists of five departments: Mathematics, Statistics and Computer Science, Physics and Astronomy, Chemistry, Biochemistry and Pharmacy, Biology and Geosciences. The Faculty is characterized by interdisciplinarity, which is reflected in the affiliated Research Competence Centers, National Centres of Competence in Research and Interfaculty Research Cooperation.

Studying at the Faculty of Philosophy and Natural Sciences is characterized by high quality teaching and internationally recognized research. In teaching, the Faculty offers a variety of study programes at Bachelor, Master and Doctoral level. Graduate Schools accompany the PhD programs and specialized Master programs.

Einstein Lectures

This year’s lecture series explores the world of numbers and the surprising connections between mathematics, history, and culture. Fields Medallist Manjul Bhargava takes audiences on a three-evening journey from magical numbers and squares and mathematical ideas reaching back thousands of years to current questions in number theory and the connections between mathematics, poetry, and rhythm: from October 19 to 21, 2026, in the main building of the University of Bern.

Monday, 19 October 2026, 7:30 p.m. - 142'857: A Magical Number Everyone Should Know
Tuesday, 20 October 2026, 5:15 p.m. - Magic Squares, Cubes, and Hypercubes: From Ancient Origins to Recent Advances
Wednesday, 21 October 2026, 7:30 p.m. - Poetry, Drumming, and Mathematics: The Shared Creativity of Rhyme, Rhythm, and Reason

Putting Switzerland’s 2026 Drought into Perspective

The effects of the 2026 drought are still visible everywhere. Researchers at the University of Bern have compared droughts in the 21st century with those of the past 600 years. Their findings show that the risk of drought is increasing sharply as climate change progresses.

Astronomy

CHEOPS Space Mission extended

Following the remarkable scientific achievements of the space telescope CHEOPS, the European Space Agency (ESA) has decided to extend the mission once again. The space telescope, which was built at the University of Bern and whose science operations center is located at the University of Geneva, will now continue to operate until the end of 2029. This decision underscores the outstanding performance and scientific value of CHEOPS in exoplanet research.

June 11, 2026
