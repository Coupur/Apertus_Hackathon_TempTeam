The major societal challenges of the next 50 years, such as climate change, water, energy and raw material supply, and dealing with natural disasters, require knowledge of earth sciences ("geology"). The necessary understanding of the formation processes of minerals, rocks, mountains and their complex interactions with the hydrosphere and climate is based on the multidisciplinary study of the Earth.

Our Institute of Geological Sciences performs fundamental research in the origin and evolution of the Earth, and applied research in natural hazards, natural resource/energy supply, and waste disposal. To understand geological processes, we use state-of-the-art analytical equipment to study rocks and their components.

We offer an attractive bachelor's and master's degree program with a combination of lectures, seminars, field trips and field work in a collegial atmosphere with individual supervision.

Prize

Best Poster Award

Congratulations to Peter Alt-Epping! He received the Best Poster Award at CouFrac 2026 in Uppsala. His poster demonstrated how complex processes in fractured rocks can be simulated using computer models. He applies these models to research on natural hydrogen, CO₂ storage and geothermal energy.

Unique diamond collection for research

The University of Bern has received a collection of around 29,000 rough diamonds donated by Dr Jeffrey W. Harris and his wife Francesca Harris. The collection offers researcher Suzette Timmerman new opportunities to investigate the evolution of the Earth's mantle and plate tectonics. The donation was also featured by Swiss media outlets TeleBärn and Berner Zeitung.

Swiss lakes reveal their secrets

Using high-resolution 3D mapping, Michael Strupler and his team are revealing hidden features on the floor of Lake Oeschinen. These structures preserve evidence of past rockfalls and provide valuable information for understanding natural hazards, including rockfall-generated tsunami waves.

Workshop on January 13-14, 2027

Hydrogeochemical characterisation of thermal fluids and reservoirs

Geothermal energy represents a reliable and renewable source of heat and electricity. In this workshop, we examine how fluid geochemistry can be used for reservoir characterisation, sustainable exploitation as well as exploration of hydrothermal systems. Presentations will include theoretical lectures as well as numerous case studies.
The workshop will take place on 13–14 January 2027 at the University of Lausanne. Further information can be found in the programme flyer. Registration deadline: December 21, 2026.

Bachelor's Degree in Earth Sciences / Geology

If you enjoy observing natural phenomena, analyzing them and developing solutions to problems, geology is the right choice for you. Geology offers an exciting combination of natural science subjects and combines practical work both in the laboratory and in nature.

Bern-Fribourg Master's in Earth Sciences

In the Master's program in cooperation with the University of Fribourg, you can specialize in many different earth science disciplines according to your interests and preferences. Our graduates have a wide range of career prospects as geologists.
