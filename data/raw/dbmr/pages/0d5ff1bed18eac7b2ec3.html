The Department for BioMedical Research (DBMR) of the Faculty of Medicine of the University of Bern, led by Prof. Mark A. Rubin, MD, was established in 1994 by the University of Bern and the Inselspital (Bern University Hospital). The DBMR is divided in 14 Research Programs with about 100 participating individual labs and several Independent Research Labs whose research spans across all biomedical fields. To realize its mission to bridge the gap between bench and bedside, the DBMR promotes an integrative perspective to clinical research with a strong emphasis in the development of translational approaches, the use of omics and other cutting-edge technologies, and extensive interaction and collaboration between laboratory-based and patient-oriented clinical research. The DBMR is also committed to fostering the careers of young academics.

Upcoming Events

Research

Sleep–wake states regulation: balance between genetically encoded stability and functional flexibility of hypothalamic neurons

Sleep and wakefulness are controlled by hypothalamic networks, but the stability of individual neurons was unknown. Using longitudinal recordings in freely moving mice, a team led by the Adamantidis lab (ZEN Program) has published a new study in Nature Communications showing that single-neuron preferences shifted across days, while the distribution of active neurons in each sleep state remained stable. Although sleep deprivation minimally affected the selectivity of these activity patterns, the sleep-promoting drug diazepam recruited NREM sleep-active cells that were previously inactive or wake-active. These findings indicate that while individual neurons exhibit dynamic, state-dependent shifts in their activity, the overall organization of sleep-wake neural populations remains stable.

Fecal transplantation: new study shows how to do it better

The Yilmaz Lab (Systems Biomedicine of Cellular Development and Signaling in Health and Disease Program) demonstrate that the key to the success of a fecal transplant is not the diversity or the dose of the transplanted bacteria, but whether the “good” bacterial strains establish themselves. The study, supported by the Swiss National Science Foundation, the IBDNet and BCPM was published in Cell Reports. They report that although donor species frequently appear, only specific lineages achieve stable strain integration. Repeated perturbation exposes intrinsic evolutionary constraints that limit durable colonization within the human gut ecosystem. The results suggest that future microbiome treatments and the screening of stool donors should no longer be evaluated solely based on species diversity, but rather specifically for suitable bacterial strains.

Dual sleep and memory roles in thalamo-cortical stroke

A new study shows that strokes in the thalamus disrupt sleep and memory by altering key brain rhythms. Using a novel mouse model, a team led by the Gutierrez Herrera lab (ZEN Program) found that targeted sound stimulation during deep sleep restored sleep stability, brain connectivity, and cognitive function. These results, now published in STROKE, reveal the brain’s capacity for sleep-dependent recovery after injury.

AI-based model calculates the risk of postoperative infection immediately after surgery

A research team from the Department of Visceral Surgery and Medicine at Inselspital and the Systems Biomedicine of Cellular Development and Signaling in Health and Disease Program at the DBMR has developed and validated a machine-learning model integrating intraoperative vital-sign dynamics to predict postoperative infections immediately at the end of surgery. This approach allows patients who are in risk of developing pos-operative complications to receive early care and in a targeted manner. The results were recently published in npj Digital Medicine and highlighted in the lastest issue of the Uni Aktuell.

SNSF Project Grant to optimize clinical protocols for pediatric heart transplantation

Congratulations to Prof. Sarah Longnus, on the approval of the SNSF Project “Ex-situ heart perfusion (ESHP) in donation after circulatory death (DCD) heart transplantation: New options for optimizing heart preservation with state-of-the-art cardiac imaging”. The overall research goals are to identify clinically relevant approaches to optimize clinical protocols for DCD heart transplantation and ESHP, ultimately to improve outcomes for all patients with advanced heart failure, but with a particular emphasis on pediatric patients, for whom no technology permitting access to these options for increased transplantation possibilities is available.

Prof. Mark Rubin among the most cited Swiss medical researchers

Prof. Mark Rubin, Cancer Therapy Resistance Program and DBMR director, is the 3rd most cited Swiss researcher (worldwide ranking 309) according to the Research.com ranking "Best Medicine Scientists 2026". The 5th edition of the Research.com Best Medicine Scientists Ranking is grounded in bibliometric data primarily sourced from OpenAlex and CrossRef, and only researchers with a D-index of at least 70 were included in the list. The data for the current ranking were collected in mid-January 2026.

People

Augspach Lab has joined the DBMR

We are delighted to announce that Anke Augspach has been appointed Assistant Professor after securing competitive third-party funding. She has joined the Translational Cancer Research Program, where her lab focuses on the minor spliceosome, a unique RNA splicing machinery with elevated activity in cancer. Their research aims to translate these biological insights into innovative therapeutic and diagnostic strategies for prostate and breast cancer. We warmly welcome Prof. Augspach to the DBMR and wish her lab every success!

Halazonetis Lab has joined the DBMR

The Halazonetis Lab focuses on DNA Replication Stress and Cancer, namely the mechanisms by which oncogenes induce DNA replication stress, the types of mutations present in cancers, what fraction of these mutations can be attributed to oncogene-induced DNA replication stress, with the ultimate goal of identifying weak spots that can be leveraged for the development of better cancer therapies. We are delighted to have Prof. Halazonetis and his team joining the DBMR and wish all the success to the lab.

Prof. Christoph Schlapbach has been promoted to Full Professor of Dermatology

We congratulate the promotion of Prof. Christoph Schlapbach, research leader in Translational Immunology program and Director and Chief Physician of the Department of Dermatology at the Inselspital, to Full Professor of Dermatology! His research focuses on T-cell biology and inflammatory skin diseases and to the development of new therapeutic approaches.

Prof. Matúš Medo conferred the title of Associate Professor

We are delighted to announce that Prof. Matúš Medo, research leader in Translational Cancer Research program and Data Scientist at the University Clinic for Radiation Oncology at Inselspital, has been promoted to Associate Professor! His current research focuses on the development and evaluation of modern methods for analyzing biological data. With his appointment, the University of Bern recognizes Matúš Medo's outstanding scientific contributions, his methodological innovation, and his interdisciplinary commitment at the interface of physics, computer science, biology, and medicine.

Prof. Antoine Adamantidis has been promoted to Full Professor of System Neurophysiology

We congratulate the promotion of Prof. Antoine Adamantidis (ZEN Program) to Full Professor of System Neurophysiology at the University of Bern. This promotion recognizes his outstanding contributions to research and teaching, as well as his internationally recognized expertise in the field of system neurophysiology. His research on sleep-wake networks and neural circuits has significantly contributed to the international understanding of sleep processes.
