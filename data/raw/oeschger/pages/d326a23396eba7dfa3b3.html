Start of NCCR Clim+: change in OCCR presidency

The Swiss National Science Foundation (SNSF) has recently approved funding of six new National Centres of Competence in Research (NCCR), among them the NCCR Clim+. CLIM+ unites expertise from both the natural and the social sciences to support Switzerland’s transformation in the face of increasing climate extremes. 

The ETH Zurich and the University of Bern are jointly leading this new Centre. Karin Ingold, former OCCR President and head of the Policy Analysis and Environmental Governance Group (PEGO), is co-directing CLIM+ with Sonia I. Seneviratne from ETH Zurich. Their deputies are Olivia Romppainen-Martius, who leads the OCCR’s Mobiliar Group for Climate Impact Research, and David N. Bresch from ETH Zurich.

From NCCR to OCCR

Karin is a veteran climate researcher, looking back at one of her earliest academic positions as research fellow at the then still young NCCR Climate which, after 2007, coalesced into the Oeschger Centre for Climate Change Research. Now, two decades later, she oversees her “own” NCCR with Clim+. 

Karin recalls the two and half year long and often difficult process to create this new NCCR from scratch: “It requires a lot of determination, optimism, and perseverance.” She and her team had to develop lasting structures and drum up financial support for an organization that might have never seen the light of day. Now that it finally has been greenlit, the real work is about to begin: The NCCR Clim+ is scheduled to start operations in May 2026. 

Stefan Brönnimann takes over as OCCR President

As Karin Ingold has taken over as Co-Director of the new NCCR Clim+, she stepped down as OCCR President on 1 May 2026. She was replaced by Vice President Stefan Brönnimann.

Stefan is another OCCR veteran: Since 2010, he has been a professor of climatology at the Institute of Geography. He's also the head of the OCCR's Climatology group. His research topics encompass the reconstruction of weather and climate during past ca. 400 years, the combination of early instrumental data, proxies and climate models, climate dynamics and large-scale variability, and historical climatology. Currently, Stefan heads the UrbanLab Viererfeld project. The project conducts transdisciplinary and multi-perspective research on how the ecological and social sustainability goals of the City of Bern can be achieved during and after the construction of the new Viererfeld and Mittelfeld neighborhood. 

Over the course of his long career, Stefan has been awarded numerous times for his research and been appointed to many scientific institutions, including most recently the Swiss GAW / GCOS Scientific Steering Committee, which he's chaired since October 2025. 

New OCCR Director appointed

As we have already reported earlier this year, there are also major changes taking place within the OCCR Directorate: Martin Grosjean will retire on 31 January 2027 and step down as the Director of the OCCR and the Graduate School of Climate Sciences. The University of Bern has recently appointed a successor: Hendrik Vogel, currently the head of the Sedimentary Geochemistry group.
