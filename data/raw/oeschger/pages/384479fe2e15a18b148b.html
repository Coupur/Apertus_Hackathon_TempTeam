SNSF Grant for ECO-ICE

The Swiss National Science Foundation (SNSF) has awarded a grant of 3.5 million Swiss Francs to ECO-ICE: The international project explores so called refugia, sanctuaries of biodiversity during the last Ice Age. Despite their importance for maintaining biodiversity during adverse climatic periods, the understanding of refugia remains limited.

ECO-ICE aims to shed light by pooling the expertise of the Paleoecology group (led by Willy Tinner), the Paleogenomics and Vegetation Modelling group (Christoph Schwörer), and Oliver Heiri, an OCCR adjunct researcher at the University of Basel. The project will study sediments preserved at ten sites across Europe, including the shores of Lake Thun (as seen in the photo).

Read the whole story on the website of the Institute of Plant Sciences.
