At the Institute of Biochemistry and Molecular Medicine (IBMM), we investigate structure, function and pharmacology of membrane proteins. We combine chemical synthesis (Lochner), chemical biology/analytics (Gertsch) with electrophysiology (Abriel, Peinelt), structural biology (Fotiadis, Kukulski) with computational biology (Lemmin). A strong focus is put on the roles of membrane transporters, ion channels and receptors in human disease. For instance, human ex vivo and genetically-modified mouse models serve to study such membrane proteins in different diseases like cancer (Peinelt), cardiac disorders (Abriel), preeclampsia (Albrecht) and neuropsychiatric disorders (Gertsch).

The IBMM drives and participates in major national collaborative initiatives. From 2010 to 2022, the IBMM served as the Leading House for NCCR TransCure, advancing membrane transporter research from fundamental discovery toward early-stage drug development. At present, the IBMM is involved in the Dubochet Center for Imaging (DCI), a flagship cryo-EM initiative by the Universities of Bern, Geneva, Lausanne, and the EPFL. At the DCI Bern, IBMM researchers contribute as scientific advisors and users in single particle cryo-EM and cellular cryo-electron tomography. 

The IBMM directorate is composed of Dimitrios Fotiadis (Managing Director and Co-Director Finances), Jürg Gertsch (Deputy Managing Director & Co-Director Infrastructure and Research), Christine Peinelt (Co-Director Teaching), and Wanda Kukulski (Co-Director Safety, Quality, and Equal Opportunities).

Leonie Anton is becoming a group leader at EMBL Grenoble

The IBMM congratulates our postdoctoral researcher Leonie Anton on becoming a group leader at EMBL Grenoble!
The European Molecular Biology Laboratory (EMBL) is Europe’s flagship for life sciences and an international organisation supported by more than 30 member countries, including Switzerland. Leonie will establish her independent group at their Grenoble site to study host-pathogen interactions and cell-autonomous immunity using in situ cryo-electron tomography. We applaud Leonie for this achievement and wish her all the best with starting her lab!

SNSF-Interview with Prof. Christiane Albrecht

There is little scientific evidence about how substances such as medications cross the placenta to the baby. A publication co-funded by the SNSF presents a realistic model to help understand this.
The biomedical scientist Christiane Albrecht and her team has now developed a lab model that replicates transport processes in the human placenta better than previous methods.

Stephan Hirschi start his independent research as an Assistant Professor at the IBMM

Stephan Hirschi has received a Scholarship from the Professor Dr Max Cloëtta Foundation to start his independent research as an Assistant Professor at the IBMM. His lab will investigate the molecular mechanisms of cholesterol and lipid transport in the Malaria pathogen Plasmodium falciparum. This work could lead to the development of new anti-malaria drugs.
