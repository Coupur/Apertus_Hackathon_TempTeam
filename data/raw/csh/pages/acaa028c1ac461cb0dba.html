Korean Journalists visit Bern's Space Research Hub

On Wednesday, 25 March 2026, the University of Bern welcomed a delegation of Korean journalists for a visit to the space research facilities in Bern and Zimmerwald. The visit was organized by Presence Switzerland and the Embassy of Switzerland in the Republic of Korea as preparation for the Swiss-Korean Innovation week "Unlocking Space – Ideas in Orbit."

Last Wednesday, the Center for Space and Habitability (CSH) took part in welcoming a delegation of journalists from the Republic of Korea to the University of Bern, where they visited our space research facilities and learned about the Bernese activities in space.

In a first round of presentations, Prof. Dr. Nicolas Thomas, Director of NCCR PlanetS, talked about the different space-related fields at the University of Bern, historic space missions with Bernese involvement such as Apollo, active missions like JUICE and BepiColombo, and introduced current projects we are actively working on for future missions like Comet Interceptor and RAMSES. He also highlighted the strong collaboration between Bern and space agencies like ESA, NASA and JAXA, the Swiss industry, and European and international institutes.

PD Dr. Martin Rubin, senior researcher at the Space Research and Planetary Sciences Division (WP) at the University of Bern, further explained the current work on Comet Interceptor, the special case this mission presents, showcased previous work on comets, like with Rosetta, and offered our visitors the chance to experience what a comet smells like.

Prof. em. Dr. Silvio Decurtins, President of the International Foundation High-Altitude Research Stations Jungfraujoch and Gornergrat (HFSJG), presented the work conducted in the Bernese mountains, its history and its collaboration with the Gwangju Institute of Science and Technology (GIST), Republic of Korea, which joined the foundation in 2024. Prof. André Stefanov, group leader of the Quantum Optics Lab at the University of Bern, further elaborated on the scientific work at HFSJG and the collaboration with GIST on free-space projects such as the Inter Satellite Laser (ISL).

In a final talk of the first round, Dr. Andrea Fortier, CHEOPS Mission Manager, presented the Bernese space telescope CHEOPS. A joint Swiss-ESA-led mission, the satellite telescope was developed at the University of Bern, which is also leading the mission consortium, made up of Switzerland and 10 European countries. Following her presentation, the journalists also had the opportunity to visit the CHEOPS model showcased at the Exact Sciences building of the University.

A tour through our lab and workshop facilities rounded off the first part of their visit. The journalists experienced first-hand how the University of Bern is involved in space research from the vision to mission. Test engineer Vincent Dor led a tour through the laboratory, where our guests were introduced to the testing facilities for space instruments like our vacuum chambers, and the shaker lab, where they got to experience the strong vibrations needed to ensure instruments will not break during a rocket launch.

Second-year Polymechanics apprentice Luca Gautschi gave a tour through the mechanical workshop situated at the Exact Sciences building where he and his colleagues are educated and actively contribute to space missions as part of their education, providing insight into the apprenticeship track at the University of Bern and the contributions of the workshop to the space research conducted in Bern.

In the evening, the delegation of journalists visited the Observatory in Zimmerwald, which is managed and operated by the Astronomical Institute (AIUB) at the University of Bern. PhD candidate Amelie Koch and Dr. Jonas Zbinden, Postdoctoral fellow at AIUB, introduced our guests to the facilities in Zimmerwald and showcased the research conducted there.

Lastly, Dr. Timm-Emanuel Riesen, Director of the Stellarium Gornergrat, presented the work of the Stellarium, which is an outreach facility located within the Observatory Gornergrat South of the HFSJG. The Stellarium is dedicated to making astronomy and space science accessible to students and the general public and providing pedagogical programs for astronomical observation.

This visit was organized by Presence Switzerland, a unit of the Swiss Federal Department of Foreign Affairs (FDFA), dedicated to the presentataion of Switzerland on an international scale, and the Embassy of Switzerland in the Republic of Korea.

We greatly appreciate this opportunity to showcase Swiss cutting-edge research in space science to the delegation. Thank you to the journalists for your time and interest in what we do at the University of Bern, and for your insightful questions and engaging conversations. Further, we would like to express our sincere gratitude to Presence Switzerland and the Embassy of Switzerland in the Republic of Korea for this opportunity. Bernese researchers are looking forward to strengthening the ties by participating in the Swiss-Korean Innovaiton week in June 2026.
