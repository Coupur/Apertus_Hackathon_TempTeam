ESA appoints CSH Scientist Dr. Valentin Bickel as Guest Investigator for Mars Trace Gas Orbiter Mission

With the tenth anniversary of the arrival of the Trace Gas Orbiter at Mars in mind, ESA selected new guest investigators to boost the mission’s scientific output. Dr. Valentin Bickel, senior research assistant at the Center for Space and Habitability (CSH), University of Bern, will continue his study of Mars’ dynamic dust devils through this appointment.

Exactly ten years ago, ESA’s ExoMars Trace Gas Orbiter arrived in Mars orbit. Following the ten-year anniversary of the mission’s launch in March 2026, the European Space Agency (ESA) announced a new opportunity for interdisciplinary scientists and guest investigators to join the mission dedicated to the study of trace gases in Mars’ atmosphere that could result from geological – or possibly biological – processes.

Guest Investigators (GIs) are scientists selected by ESA through a competitive Announcement of Opportunity (AO) to join specific exploration missions. GIs use the data derived by one or several instruments to perform scientific investigations and boost the scientific output of the overall mission.

Today, we are thrilled to announce that among the newly selected researchers is CSH Scientist Dr. Valentin Bickel. As GI for TGO, Valentin will use a computer vision-driven data processing pipeline to systematically search for dust devil activity across the Red Planet – transient vortices that paint mesmerizing tracks all over the martian surface.

“The shape of these tracks provides clues about the wind speed at a given place and point in time,” says Valentin. The investigation will use dust devils and their tracks as ‘weather vanes’ to better characterize Mars’ dynamic, lowermost atmosphere as a key component of Mars’ overall weather and climate.

In his role as GI, Valentin will closely work with several instrument teams, such as the CaSSIS team (led out of Bern) and the Mars Express HRSC team (led out of Berlin, Germany).

CaSSIS – the Colour and Stereo Surface Imaging System – is the main imaging system onboard the ExoMars Trace Gas Orbiter. The instrument is a high resolution imaging system designed to complement the data acquired by the other payload on TGO while also enhancing our knowledge of the surface of Mars by extending the observations of the High Resolution Imaging Experiment (HiRISE) which is currently orbiting Mars onboard NASA’s Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (MRO).
