Ready to discover new worlds? Planet Hunters CHEOPS is live!

ESA launched Planet Hunters CHEOPS, a new citizen science project that invites the public to help search for exoplanets using data from the CHEOPS space telescope, which was developed at the University of Bern, where mission management lies with the Center for Space and Habitability (CSH).

What if the next exoplanet discovery started with you? Could you help find a new world?

Since taking first light in 2020, ESA’s CHEOPS mission has observed thousands of stars, generating a treasure trove of light curves that may contain the signatures of undiscovered exoplanets. The European Space Agency now launched Planet Hunters CHEOPS, a new citizen science project that lets anyone help search for planets beyond our Solar System.

In this project, interested people can explore high-precision light curves from the CHEOPS space telescope and help search for previously unnoticed exoplanet signals of background stars observed together with the prime targets. By spotting tiny dips in a star’s brightness, volunteers can identify possible planetary transits and unusual patterns that automated methods may miss.

Hosted on Zooniverse, Planet Hunters CHEOPS requires no astronomy experience—just curiosity and a few minutes of your time. Anyone interested to contribute to possible new planet discoveries is invited to take part. These classifications of real CHEOPS data support scientific research and help build training datasets for future machine-learning tools.

Join the hunt!


At the University of Bern, Sara Marques will lead the scientific analysis of the classifications made by volunteers. Sara is a PhD student at the Space Research and Planetary Sciences Division (WP) of the Physics Institute at the University of Bern, focusing on machine learning and planetary sciences. She contributed to other citizen science projects herself as well.
