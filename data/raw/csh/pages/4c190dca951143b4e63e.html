CSH Fellow Dr. Marrick Braam receives SNSF Ambizione Grant for Exoplanet Environment Project

With his project “Prebiotic Chemistry in Exoplanet Environments,” Marrick Braam is one of 93 selected recipients of the 2025 Ambizione Grant, an early-career funding scheme of the Swiss National Science Foundation (SNSF). This grant funds his research for the next four years.

The SNSF Ambizione Grant is aimed at early-career researchers and supports them in conducting, managing and leading an independent research project. In November 2025, 803 researchers submitted a proposal to the call, of which 93 were selected, as the SNSF announced on August 13, 2026.

Among the 93 recipients are 10 hosted at the University of Bern, bringing a total of 7.5 Mio. Swiss francs in funding to Bern, spread over four different faculties.

A CSH Fellow since September 2024, Dr. Marrick Braam officially begins his Ambizione project “Prebiotic Chemistry in Exoplanet Environments” on October 1, 2026, for which he received CHF 595’559 in funding for the next four years.  The project will include collaborations within the CSH as well as external partnerships with the Exoclimatology Theory Group and the LIFE Space Mission.

With this project, Marrick and his collaborators will investigate whether rocky exoplanet environments can support prebiotic chemistry and what these environments imply for interpreting potential biosignatures. To do so, the project connects two frontier areas of exoplanetary science: prebiotic chemistry and climate modelling.

On early Earth, the environmental conditions determined the production and surface delivery of important prebiotic precursors molecules which were crucial for the emergence of life. On exoplanets, environmental conditions including stellar radiation and lightning may similarly determine the production and delivery of precursor molecules. Since early Earth-like atmospheres may represent a significant part of an exoplanet’s history, understanding these environmental conditions is key to interpreting future observations of rocky exoplanet atmospheres.

Using coupled climate-chemistry models, Marrick aims to understand these environmental conditions and their implications for prebiotic chemistry on exoplanets. The simulated environments will help inform the science goals and instrument design of the Large Interferometer For Exoplanets (LIFE), a Swiss-led space mission designed to search for life outside the Solar System and investigate the diversity of other worlds. Overall, the project will contribute to a deeper understanding of the origin and distribution of life in the universe.

We warmly congratulate Marrick on this incredible achievement and look forward to the exciting research he will conduct in the coming years. Congrats!
