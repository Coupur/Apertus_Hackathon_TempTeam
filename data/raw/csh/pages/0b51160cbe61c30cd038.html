Current Space Missions with Bernese Involvement

The University of Bern is involved in several active space missions, from contributing scientific instruments to co-leading missions. Here you will find what missions CSH and WP are currently engaged in.

This page is currently under construction, more information will be available soon.

2023 – today | Jupiter | ESA Mission

JUICE — Mass Spectrometer NIM and Laser Altimeter GALA

The JUpiter ICy Moons Explorer (JUICE) mission is the first major mission under ESA's "Cosmic Vision 2015 – 2025." JUICE will observe the giant gas planet Jupiter and three of its largest moons—Ganymede, Callisto, and Europa. On board is the Particle Environment Package (PEP) instrument suite, which was designed at the University of Bern and assembled from international instruments. Part of PEP is the Bernese Neutral and Ion Mass Spectrometer (NIM), which will take measurements in the moons' atmospheres. The Ganymede Laser Altimeter (GALA) will perform topographic mapping and contribute to a large scale geodesy experiment.

Follow along with the JUICE Mission Highlights!

Timeline

Timeline

JUICE was launched on April 14, 2023 on an Ariane 5 rocket from Europe's Spaceport in French Guiana. It is scheduled to arrive at Jupiter in July 2031. Several flybys throughout the journey bring JUICE back near Earth. A Lunar-Earth flyby occured in August 2024 and a Venus flyby in August 2025. Another Earth flyby will occur on 28 September 2026 with a final Earth flyby scheduled for January 2029, following which JUICE will be on its final path toward Jupiter.

PEP and NIM

The Particle Environment Package (PEP) was selected by ESA to be part of the 11 scientific instruments on JUICE. PEP is a consortium of six science instruments. The University of Bern contributes to PEP with the Neutral and Ion Mass Spectrometer (NIM).

PEP's six instruments are able to measure neutral gas, thermal plasma, positive and negative ions, electrons, and energetic neutral atoms in the energy range from 1 eV to 5 MeV. The PEP instruments are organized into two groups: PEP-Lo and PEP-Hi. PEP-Lo is organised in two units, the Nadir Unit and the Zenith Unit. The University of Bern is responsible for the entire mechanical system engineering and qualification of the PEP-Lo Nadir Unit, which houses NIM and two other instruments. This includes the design and fabrication of the unit's mechanical parts, the assembly and integration of the participating instrument and the verification at system level. The University of Bern also contributed hardware to another PEP instrument (JNA).

The NIM instrument is a highly sensitive mass spectrometer for neutral atmospheric gas and ionospheric ions specifically developed for the tenuous gases of planetary exospheres. It is a time-of-flight mass spectrometer using an ion mirror for performance optimisation. It will be used to measure the chemical composition of the regular atmosphere produced by sublimation, energetic particle bombardment, and photon interaction with the surface of the icy Jovian moons.

GALA

As part of its mission, JUICE will orbit the Jovian moon Ganymede and perform a detailed investigation of it. The Ganymede Laser Altimeter (GALA) on board JUICE will perform topographic mapping and contribute to a large scale geodesy experiment. GALA was developed by DLR (Germany), but its rangefinder system with the associated software was provided by the Planetary Imaging Group of the University of Bern's Space Research and Planetary Science Division (WP). The instrument uses a similar system to BELA (BepiColombo mission), which was developed at the University of Bern.

2018 – today | Mercury | ESA and JAXA Mission

BepiColombo — Laser Altimeter BELA & Mass Spectrometer STROFIO

BepiColombo is Europe and Japan's first mission to Mercury, and the second and most complex mission ever to orbit the planet. A joint mission of the European Space Agency (ESA) and the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA), BepiColombo will investigate the small desert world of Mercury, which is the innermost and least explored planet in the inner Solar System. The mission is comprised of two science orbiters – the Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO) and the Mercury Magnetospheric Orbiter (Mio) – packed with scientific instruments to thoroughly study the planet. On board of MPO are two instruments developed at the University of Bern: the BepiColombo Laser Altimeter (BELA) and STROFIO (sTart from a ROtating Field mass spectrOmeter), which will map the planets surface and analyze its exosphere, respectively.

Follow along with the BepiColombo Mission Highlights!

Timeline

Timeline

BepiColombo was launched on October 20, 2018, on an Ariane 5 rocket from Europe's Spaceport in French Guiana. It is scheduled to arrive at Mercury in late 2026 in several key arrival stages: i) September 03, 2026 – on approach to Mercury, the Mercury Transfer Module (MTM) releases the Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO) and Mercury Magnetospheric Orbiter (Mio); ii) November 21, 2026 – MPO and Mio, still joined together, are captured by Mercury's gravity into a polar orbit around the planet; iii) December 09-10, 2026 – MPO releases Mio onto its final elliptical polar orbit; iv) December 16, 2026 – MPO releases Mio's sunshield (MOSIF) and moves toward its final orbit; v) March 10, 2027 – MPO arrives in its final orbit; and vi) April 06, 2027 – begin science phase of the mission.

On its journey to Mercury, BepiColombo achieved several flybys of Earth (April 2020), Venus (October 2020, August 2021) and Mercury (October 2021, June 2022 and 2023, September and December 2024, January 2025).

BELA

The BepiColombo Laser Altimeter (BELA) is one of the most important and most sensitive experiments on board the MPO. The instrument was developed by an international consortium under the management of the University of Bern and the German Aerospace Center (DLR). The aim of BELA is to measure the shape, topography and morphology of the surface of Mercury. This information will be used to create a digital terrain model that allows quantitative exploration of the geology, the tectonics, and the age of the planet's surface.

BELA was the first laser altimeter developed for a European space mission and is an integral part of a larger geodesy and geophysics package, incorporating radio science and stereo imaging.

STROFIO

STROFIO (sTart from a ROtating Field mass spectrOmeter) is an innovative mass spectrometer developed at the Physics Institute of the University of Bern, and is one of four sensors of the SERENA (Search for Exospheric Refilling and Emitted Natural Abundances) experiment on board the MPO. SERENA provides information on the whole surface-exosphere-magnetosphere system and the involved processes, plus the interaction between energetic particles, the solar wind, micrometeorites and the interplanetary medium.

STROFIO was specifically built for Mercury's thin atmosphere and for its measurement on the MPO's orbit, where it will in-situ record the density of low-energy neutral particles within the very thin atmospheres of Mercury (exosphere) and analyze its chemical composition.
