The Department of Physics and Astronomy at the University of Bern offers a rich program of teaching and research, addressing topics of the highest scientific significance and societal relevance.

Research activities span both fundamental and applied research. Current research areas include astronomy, particle physics, theoretical physics, climate and environmental physics, planetary science, space research, biomedical photonics, microwave physics, and laser physics. In addition to conducting research using internationally recognised in-house experimental facilities, the department maintains intensive theoretical and experimental collaborations with CERN, plays a strong role in space research missions led by the European and American space agencies ESA and NASA, as well as other partners, and benefits from observing time at the European Southern Observatory (ESO).

Furthermore, the Department of Physics and Astronomy hosts the Albert Einstein Center for Fundamental Physics (AEC), one of the largest university-based research groups in Switzerland in the field of particle physics. Interdisciplinary collaborations are also fostered through the Oeschger Center for Climate Change Research (OCCR) and the Center for Space and Habitability (CSH), and the department is well integrated into the Swiss research landscape.

Study Programs and Courses

Bachelor's Studies

The Bachelor’s program provides a solid foundation in physics, astronomy, and mathematics. Students also have the opportunity to participate in exciting research projects across a range of subject areas.

Bachelor's Study Program (in German only)

Master's Studies

The Master’s program deepens and further specializes the foundational knowledge acquired during the Bachelor’s studies in two possible fields of study. The Master’s thesis provides the opportunity for independent research within one of the many research groups.

Master's Study Program

Courses

The program courses are organised by four institutes—the Institute of Applied Physics, the Institute of Theoretical Physics, the Physics Institute, and the Institute of Astronomy—and comprises lectures, practical courses, seminars, and exercises.

Doctoral Studies

Doctoral students work on current research topics in physics with the aim of generating new knowledge and advancing our understanding of nature. They collaborate closely with fellow doctoral students and researchers.

Lecture Series and Colloquium

Physics on Friday

The “Physics on Friday” lecture series is aimed at high school students and anyone with a general interest in physics.

About the lecture series

Einstein Lectures

The Einstein Lectures are given by individuals who have made outstanding contributions to fields associated with Albert Einstein's legacy. Topics in physics, mathematics, and philosophy are addressed on a rotating basis.

About the lectures

Physics Colloquium

A wide range of topics from the diverse fields of physics is discussed at an advanced academic level. While the colloquia are primarily intended for physicists, anyone with an interest in physics is welcome to attend.
